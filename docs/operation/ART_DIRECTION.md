# Operation art direction: the owner's rules

Given by the owner on 2026-11-10 with the words "For our operation keep this as the rules please." This is the standing
brief for every operation level (first: Meridian Fall, `/Game/Maps/L_Meridian`). The text below is the owner's, kept
as written. How it meets the project's other hard rules is in the notes at the end.

---

MASTER ART DIRECTION PROMPT — AAA ENVIRONMENT TRANSFORMATION

OPERATION

You are acting as a senior AAA environment art director, world builder, level designer, technical artist and Unreal
Engine 5 environment specialist.

Your assignment is to transform my existing operation level into a visually stunning, fully realised AAA-quality
sci-fi environment.

The current structures look too much like buildings under construction. They are bare, unfinished, empty and lack the
visual detail expected from a commercial AAA game.

I want you to completely elevate the environment.

Use Destiny 2 as a quality benchmark for environmental detail, visual composition, architectural identity, lighting,
atmosphere and environmental storytelling.

Do not copy Destiny 2 locations, assets or designs. Create an original visual identity

The objective is to make the player feel as though they are exploring a once-great human city that was destroyed,
abandoned and gradually consumed by an alien ecosystem.

This is a playable strike mission, not a static environment showcase.

## 1. FIRST: AUDIT THE EXISTING LEVEL

Before changing anything:

1. Inspect the existing Unreal Engine project.
2. Examine the entire operation map.
3. Identify unfinished-looking structures.
4. Identify empty spaces, repetitive buildings and bare surfaces.
5. Identify areas lacking environmental detail.
6. Examine the existing mission route and boss arena.
7. Identify opportunities for environmental storytelling.
8. Preserve existing gameplay functionality, enemy encounters, collision, navigation and mission progression.

Do not delete the existing level and start from scratch without a clear reason.

Upgrade the existing environment wherever practical.

Preserve the existing map layout and gameplay route unless changes are necessary to improve the level.

First produce a concise environment improvement plan, then begin implementation.

## 2. OVERALL VISUAL IDENTITY

The operation takes place in a ruined futuristic human city on a transformed Earth.

The city was once a sophisticated, technologically advanced human metropolis.

Something catastrophic happened.

The city has been abandoned for years, and alien vegetation and biological structures have gradually taken over.

The visual identity should combine:

* Advanced futuristic human architecture.
* Destroyed urban infrastructure.
* Alien biological growth.
* Overgrown streets.
* Abandoned military installations.
* Damaged transportation systems.
* Collapsed skyscrapers.
* Atmospheric environmental storytelling.
* Beautiful but dangerous natural environments.

The environment should feel believable, detailed and deliberately art-directed.

Avoid generic grey concrete buildings.

Avoid unfinished structures.

Avoid repetitive modular architecture that makes the city look procedurally assembled.

Every major location should have its own architectural identity.

## 3. ARCHITECTURE — MAKE THE BUILDINGS LOOK COMPLETE

This is one of the highest priorities.

Buildings must look like fully constructed buildings that have subsequently been damaged by a catastrophe.

They must NOT look like:

* Construction sites.
* Bare concrete skeletons.
* Unfinished towers.
* Empty rectangular boxes.
* Buildings made from randomly stacked cubes.
* Simple grey architectural placeholders.

Create complete architectural surfaces and believable details.

Building details

Include:

* Finished exterior wall materials.
* Windows and damaged glass.
* Window frames.
* Balconies.
* Architectural trim.
* Decorative facades.
* Rooftop equipment.
* Air-conditioning and ventilation systems.
* Electrical infrastructure.
* Pipes and conduits.
* Signage.
* Lighting fixtures.
* Doors and entrances.
* Damaged exterior panels.
* Structural support systems.
* Weathering and surface variation.
* Broken sections revealing interior spaces.

Buildings should have different heights, silhouettes, architectural styles and levels of destruction.

Some should remain mostly intact.

Others should have partially collapsed floors, damaged facades, exposed interiors and vegetation growing through them.

Create believable structural damage rather than simply removing random sections of buildings.

## 4. ENVIRONMENTAL STORYTELLING

Every part of the level should tell a story.

I want players to be able to look at the environment and understand that something happened here.

Do not scatter random decorations everywhere.

Every environmental detail should have a reason to exist.

Examples

Abandoned residential district

* Apartments with broken windows.
* Furniture visible inside damaged buildings.
* Overturned vehicles.
* Personal belongings.
* Abandoned signs.
* Plants growing through broken floors.
* Collapsed walkways.
* Old human infrastructure.

Former commercial district

* Destroyed storefronts.
* Broken advertising displays.
* Abandoned public transport.
* Damaged holographic signage.
* Overgrown pedestrian areas.
* Collapsed commercial buildings.

Military district

* Destroyed defensive barricades.
* Abandoned military vehicles.
* Damaged weapon emplacements.
* Ruined checkpoints.
* Defensive walls.
* Destroyed equipment.
* Signs of a desperate last stand.

Research and technology district

* Damaged laboratories.
* Broken containment chambers.
* Destroyed research equipment.
* Alien biological growth inside facilities.
* Energy conduits.
* Abandoned experimental machinery.

The goal is to make the player wonder what happened here.

## 5. ALIEN VEGETATION AND VINES

The alien vegetation is a defining feature of this environment.

It should look like an actual ecosystem that has been growing for years.

Do not simply place green vines on buildings.

Create an original alien plant design language.

Vegetation types

Create and use different types of alien vegetation:

* Thick climbing vines.
* Large roots penetrating concrete.
* Hanging vines from skyscrapers.
* Bioluminescent plants.
* Alien moss.
* Large organic growths.
* Strange trees.
* Fungal formations.
* Alien flowers.
* Organic tendrils.
* Glowing spores.
* Dense vegetation around abandoned structures.

Natural growth behaviour

Vegetation should grow logically:

* Through broken windows.
* Around pillars.
* Along building facades.
* Through cracks in roads.
* Around abandoned vehicles.
* Across rooftops.
* Through destroyed interiors.
* Along drainage systems.
* Around collapsed bridges.

Large vines should physically wrap around structures.

Some roots should penetrate damaged concrete.

Some buildings should be almost completely consumed by vegetation, while others should have only early signs of
growth.

Use variation in density and colour.

The vegetation should look beautiful, mysterious and slightly threatening.

## 6. COMPLETE ENVIRONMENTAL ASSETS

Replace bare surfaces and primitive placeholders with actual production-quality environment assets.

Examples include:

* Street lamps.
* Road barriers.
* Damaged vehicles.
* Abandoned transport systems.
* Street signs.
* Traffic lights.
* Broken holographic advertisements.
* Rooftop machinery.
* Electrical transformers.
* Power cables.
* Pipes.
* Ventilation units.
* Industrial machinery.
* Rubble.
* Concrete debris.
* Broken glass.
* Furniture.
* Abandoned equipment.
* Damaged security systems.
* Human infrastructure.
* Alien biological structures.

- [ ] Every asset should belong to the visual identity of the operation.

Do not rely on repeated identical objects.

Create variations in geometry, materials, damage and placement.

## 7. ROADS AND GROUND DETAIL

The streets must feel like real city streets that have been abandoned.

Include:

* Proper road surfaces.
* Lane markings.
* Cracked asphalt.
* Potholes.
* Broken sidewalks.
* Curbs.
* Drainage systems.
* Damaged road signs.
* Debris.
* Abandoned vehicles.
* Vegetation breaking through the pavement.
* Water accumulation.
* Environmental stains.
* Rubble and collapsed structures.

Use believable transitions between roads, sidewalks, buildings and surrounding terrain.

Avoid enormous flat surfaces.

## 8. INTERIOR AND EXTERIOR ENVIRONMENTS

The level should not consist entirely of exterior buildings.

Make selected buildings explorable.

Create:

* Abandoned apartment interiors.
* Destroyed office spaces.
* Military checkpoints.
* Research laboratories.
* Underground service tunnels.
* Maintenance corridors.
* Rooftop access areas.
* Underground parking structures.

Interiors should have proper floors, ceilings, walls, lighting, doors, furniture and environmental details.

Give the player reasons to look around.

Use selected interior spaces for combat encounters, exploration and environmental storytelling.

## 9. LIGHTING AND ATMOSPHERE

The lighting should be cinematic and visually impressive.

Create a dark, mysterious atmosphere without making the environment so dark that players cannot see enemies or
navigate.

Use:

* High-quality global illumination.
* Natural directional lighting.
* Volumetric fog.
* Atmospheric depth.
* Light shafts.
* Reflections.
* Localised artificial lighting.
* Alien bioluminescence.
* Subtle environmental particles.
* Dust and spores.
* Atmospheric colour grading.

Create distinct lighting compositions for different areas.

Some streets should be illuminated by sunlight passing between skyscrapers.

Others should be dark, with alien plants providing subtle illumination.

Use lighting to guide players naturally through the environment.

## 11. DESTINY-LEVEL ENVIRONMENTAL QUALITY

Use the following principles as quality benchmarks:

* Strong architectural silhouettes.
* Beautiful environmental composition.
* Detailed materials.
* Rich environmental storytelling.
* Large-scale landmarks.
* Layered geometry.
* High-quality lighting.
* Distinct visual identities for different areas.
* Believable destruction.
* Carefully placed environmental assets.
* Memorable spaces.

The player should be able to stop and admire the environment.

Every screenshot should look like it belongs to a commercially released AAA science-fiction game.

However, visual quality must never come at the expense of gameplay readability or performance.

## 12. UNREAL ENGINE 5 IMPLEMENTATION

Use appropriate Unreal Engine 5 technology.

Consider:

* Nanite for suitable high-detail static meshes.
* Lumen for global illumination and reflections.
* Virtual Shadow Maps where appropriate.
* World Partition and level streaming where useful.
* PCG Framework for controlled vegetation distribution.
* Foliage tools and instanced meshes.
* Material instances.
* Decals for damage, dirt and environmental wear.
* Niagara for spores, dust and atmospheric effects.
* Landscape tools for surrounding terrain.

Use modular assets intelligently.

Create reusable asset kits for buildings, roads, walls, vegetation and environmental props.

Do not use procedural generation as an excuse for repetitive or generic environments.

Use hand-authored placement for important landmarks and major storytelling compositions.

## 13. PERFORMANCE

This is a commercial multiplayer looter shooter.

The level must be visually rich without destroying performance.

Requirements:

* Appropriate LODs.
* Instancing for repeated objects.
* Nanite where suitable.
* Efficient materials.
* Reasonable draw calls.
* Proper collision.
* Optimised vegetation.
* Controlled shadow complexity.
* Appropriate streaming.
* Multiplayer-safe level design.

Preserve navigation meshes and enemy movement.

Maintain collision and combat functionality.

Do not introduce unnecessary performance problems through excessive detail.

## 14. STRICT QUALITY RULES

1. Never leave buildings looking unfinished.
2. Never use bare grey boxes as final architecture.
3. Never use random cubes as environmental decoration.
4. Never scatter vegetation without considering natural growth.
5. Never repeat the same building design everywhere.
6. Never make every street look identical.
7. Never sacrifice environmental storytelling for random detail.
8. Never destroy existing gameplay functionality.
9. Never replace important level geometry without checking its dependencies.
10. Never claim AAA quality simply because the level contains Nanite and Lumen.

Every major structure must be visually complete.

Every district must have a recognisable identity.

Every major environment should feel intentionally designed.

## 15. IMPLEMENTATION WORKFLOW

Work in stages.

Stage 1: Audit the existing operation and document the problems.

Stage 2: Establish the visual art direction and architectural language.

Stage 3: Upgrade the buildings and major landmarks.

Stage 4: Add environmental destruction, debris and abandoned human infrastructure.

Stage 5: Build the alien vegetation ecosystem and integrate it naturally into the architecture.

Stage 6: Upgrade streets, interiors and smaller environmental assets.

Stage 7: Improve lighting, fog, materials and atmosphere.

Stage 8: Refine the mission route and boss arena.

Stage 9: Test gameplay, navigation, collision and multiplayer compatibility.

Stage 10: Profile performance and fix visual inconsistencies.

After each stage, compile or test the project as appropriate.

Maintain backups and do not overwrite the original operation.

## FINAL OBJECTIVE

Transform the existing operation into a fully realised, visually rich, cinematic AAA sci-fi ruined city.

I want a world that looks like an ancient human civilization has fallen and an alien ecosystem has taken its place.

The architecture must be complete.

The vegetation must feel alive.

The ruins must tell stories.

The environment must have depth, atmosphere and identity.

The player must feel like they are exploring a real place rather than walking through a collection of unfinished
game-development assets.

Do not just add more objects. Improve the artistic quality, composition, architecture and storytelling of the entire
environment.

## How this meets the project's other hard rules (Claude's notes, not the owner's text)

- The brief as received has no section 10 (it goes from 9 to 11): nothing was dropped here.
- Every model and texture is still made by our own Blender scripts (`tools/blender`): the kits the brief asks for
  (buildings, roads, vegetation, props, vehicles) are built there, never bought or imported from elsewhere.
- The other game is named here only as the owner's quality benchmark. Nothing of it goes into the game: no names,
  terms, text, shapes or layouts.
- Abandoned and wrecked vehicles are asked for in the operation. (The standing rule to keep people and vehicles out
  of Nexus Prime is about the city hub and still holds there.)
- "Maintain backups and do not overwrite the original operation": the backup is git. Commit the operation as it
  stands before a stage begins, and each stage after it passes its tests.
- What the level builder must keep through every stage: the route and stages in `tools/operation/meridian_plan.py`,
  the exact collision meshes (`MR_<zone>_solid`), the nav legs and spawn audit (`SOCaptureSeries 1 9` on L_Meridian),
  one area loaded at a time, 60 fps on a mid-range PC.
