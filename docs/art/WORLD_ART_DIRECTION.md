# World art direction: the owner's master brief

Given by the owner on 2026-11-16 with the words "Please remember and save this and consult it to ensure our world is
triple a and fits with the story." It is the standing direction for the whole game's atmosphere, lighting and world
identity: every level, sky, weather, light, post-process, colour grade, environment and faction. The text between
the rules below is the owner's, kept as written (only the headings' Markdown marks are added). How it meets the
story and the project's other rules is in the notes at the end, which are ours, not the owner's.

---

MASTER ART DIRECTION PROMPT — GAME ATMOSPHERE, LIGHTING & WORLD IDENTITY

## 1. CORE CREATIVE VISION

Establish a cohesive AAA-quality science-fiction visual identity for the game.

The overall atmosphere must be:

* Hopeful, adventurous and immersive.
* Futuristic, technologically advanced and visually sophisticated.
* Mysterious, encouraging exploration and discovery.
* War-torn without being depressing or excessively dark.
* Cinematic, beautiful and visually memorable.

The fundamental artistic principle: The world should look beautiful even when it is dangerous.

Do NOT make the entire universe look like a dark, depressing, creepy or post-apocalyptic wasteland.

Avoid excessive grey environments, monotonous brown landscapes, permanent fog, oppressive darkness, lifeless vegetation and overly bleak colour grading.

The player should feel that there is an enormous universe worth exploring, not that everything is already dead.

The visual quality should aim for premium modern AAA science-fiction games, with carefully composed environments, sophisticated materials, realistic lighting, atmospheric depth and exceptional environmental detail.

Use Destiny-like environmental richness, Halo-like sense of scale and natural beauty, and the visual sophistication of modern cinematic science fiction as broad quality references. Do not copy their specific assets or designs.

## 2. STORY CONTEXT — IMPORTANT FOR VISUAL DESIGN

The visual direction must reflect the game’s actual narrative.

The alien empire originally consisted of multiple different species united under one emperor.

Humanity initially suffers a devastating invasion:

* Alien forces attack the Sol system.
* Humanity loses planet after planet.
* Alien forces approach Earth.
* The Battle of Earth begins.
* Shortly afterward, the emperor is assassinated.
* The empire fractures into separate species-based governments and competing empires.
* No major species is willing to submit to another.
* Alien fleets begin fighting each other for control of the former empire.
* Many alien forces withdraw from Sol to fight for territory and power elsewhere.
* Those remaining are preoccupied with internal warfare, defending their territories or consolidating power.
* Humanity gains an opportunity to survive, rebuild and eventually fight back.

The visual environment must communicate this changing political and military situation.

The universe is not simply humanity versus one unified alien enemy. It is a universe of competing civilizations, fractured alliances, independent military powers and ongoing conflicts.

Environmental storytelling should reflect this everywhere.

## 3. GLOBAL LIGHTING PHILOSOPHY

Lighting is one of the highest priorities.

Create realistic, cinematic lighting that maintains excellent visibility and environmental beauty.

### Daytime lighting

* Natural, bright sunlight.
* Physically believable directional lighting.
* Soft, realistic shadows.
* Warm sunlight with subtle golden highlights.
* Beautiful blue skies and realistic cloud formations.
* Proper global illumination and ambient lighting.
* Realistic atmospheric perspective.
* Balanced exposure that preserves environmental detail.

Avoid washed-out environments, excessively bright highlights and artificially dark shadows.

### Nighttime lighting

* Use deep navy blue, dark blue and subtle atmospheric tones rather than completely black environments.
* Moonlight should illuminate landscapes and architectural silhouettes.
* Artificial lights should provide environmental depth.
* Human installations should have functional lighting.
* Alien technology can introduce unusual light sources and colours.
* Preserve gameplay visibility without making nighttime look like daytime.

### Interior lighting

* Human residential spaces: warm, comfortable and inviting.
* Human military facilities: clean neutral-white and subtle cool lighting.
* Laboratories: sophisticated white and cool-blue illumination.
* Hangars: industrial lighting with realistic overhead fixtures.
* Alien structures: species-specific lighting identities.
* Emergency environments: controlled red warning illumination.

### Lighting quality requirements

Use:

* Global illumination.
* Physically based lighting.
* High-quality shadow mapping.
* Realistic ambient occlusion.
* Volumetric lighting where appropriate.
* Physically based exposure.
* Realistic reflections.
* Controlled bloom.
* High-quality atmospheric scattering.

Avoid excessive bloom, exaggerated lens flares, permanent volumetric fog and excessive colour saturation.

## 4. PLANETARY ENVIRONMENT DIVERSITY

Every planet must have its own distinct visual identity.

Do not generate every planet using the same colour palette, lighting, vegetation, architecture or environmental composition.

Create several major biome families.

### A. Lush alien worlds

* Vast green forests.
* Unusual alien vegetation.
* Rivers, lakes and waterfalls.
* Enormous trees and distinctive plant formations.
* Beautiful sunlight filtering through vegetation.
* Unusual wildlife and alien ecosystems.
* Ancient ruins and unexplored structures.

These environments should feel beautiful, alive and inviting while retaining dangerous areas.

### B. Desert worlds

* Vast golden sand dunes.
* Large rock formations.
* Ancient buried civilizations.
* Abandoned alien installations.
* Natural erosion and geological diversity.
* Warm sunlight and spectacular sunsets.
* Dust storms as occasional weather events, not permanent environmental conditions.

### C. Frozen worlds

* Massive glaciers.
* Frozen oceans.
* Snow-covered mountains.
* Ice caves.
* Abandoned research installations.
* Blue atmospheric lighting.
* Snowstorms and changing weather.

The environments should feel cold and visually beautiful rather than uniformly dark.

### D. Urban worlds

* Massive alien cities.
* Towering futuristic architecture.
* Elevated transportation systems.
* Large industrial complexes.
* Dense architectural compositions.
* Distinct alien architectural languages.
* Civilian and military infrastructure.
* Enormous structures visible in the distance.

### E. Volcanic worlds

* Volcanic mountain ranges.
* Black rock formations.
* Glowing minerals.
* Lava formations and geothermal activity.
* Industrial alien installations.
* Strong contrast between dark geological surfaces and warm orange illumination.

### F. Additional environments

Allow procedural generation to support:

* Grasslands.
* Wetlands.
* Tropical environments.
* Mountain ranges.
* Canyons.
* Coastal regions.
* Underground cave systems.
* Alien oceans.
* Crystal formations.
* Abandoned megastructures.

Biome variety should be substantial enough that exploration feels rewarding.

## 5. EARTH — HUMANITY’S HOME

Earth must represent humanity’s survival, resilience and future.

Do not turn the entire planet into a destroyed wasteland.

Human settlements should feel technologically advanced, populated and functional.

Include:

* Enormous defensive walls surrounding protected settlements.
* Advanced military installations.
* Civilian settlements.
* Restored natural environments.
* Green parks and vegetation.
* Functional transportation infrastructure.
* Large spacecraft hangars.
* Military command centers.
* Advanced human architecture.
* Recovered alien technology integrated into human infrastructure.

Create a strong visual distinction between protected human territory and hostile territories outside the defensive perimeter.

Protected settlements should feel safe, technologically sophisticated and inhabited.

Outside the walls, environments can show the consequences of the invasion:

* Destroyed military installations.
* Abandoned vehicles.
* Alien structures.
* Crashed spacecraft.
* Battle-damaged infrastructure.
* Overgrown areas.
* Remnants of previous human settlements.

However, preserve environmental beauty and colour variation even in devastated regions.

## 6. ALIEN FACTIONS AND THE FRACTURED EMPIRE

Alien civilizations must have their own recognizable identities.

Do not make all alien architecture, weapons, ships and installations look alike.

Each species-based empire should have its own:

* Architectural style.
* Construction materials.
* Technology.
* Weapon aesthetics.
* Military infrastructure.
* Colour palette.
* Ship designs.
* Defensive structures.
* Environmental presence.

The fractured empire should be visible through environmental storytelling.

Examples:

1. Two alien species actively fighting each other.
2. Captured installations displaying a different faction’s insignia.
3. Destroyed spacecraft belonging to opposing species.
4. Military checkpoints and defensive fortifications.
5. Abandoned installations following faction withdrawals.
6. Alien ships fighting in planetary atmospheres.
7. Different factions competing for resources and strategic locations.
8. Military bases established around valuable alien technology.

Make the world feel like an ongoing interstellar conflict rather than a collection of disconnected enemy camps.

Some alien factions may be aggressive toward humanity, others may be neutral, and others may eventually become potential allies.

## 7. STORY-BASED ENVIRONMENTAL EVOLUTION

The environment should reflect the progression of the narrative.

### Phase 1: The invasion

Visual characteristics:

* Enormous alien fleets in planetary skies.
* Military mobilization.
* Human spacecraft and defensive systems.
* Burning spacecraft and damaged infrastructure.
* Large-scale battles.
* Alien invasion forces occupying human territory.

The environments should feel intense and cinematic without relying on permanent darkness.

### Phase 2: The Battle of Earth

This is the most visually intense period.

Include:

* Enormous spacecraft battles.
* Alien landing zones.
* Human defensive installations.
* Explosions and battle damage.
* Military convoys.
* Destroyed spacecraft.
* Large-scale environmental destruction.

Use dramatic lighting, but preserve environmental readability.

### Phase 3: The imperial fracture

The alien civil war becomes a defining feature of the universe.

Show:

* Alien fleets attacking one another.
* Competing military installations.
* Abandoned imperial structures.
* New faction territories.
* Destroyed spacecraft from internal battles.
* Different species establishing independent governments.

The atmosphere should transition from a unified invasion to a complicated interstellar conflict.

### Phase 4: Humanity’s recovery

Humanity begins rebuilding.

Show:

* Reconstructed settlements.
* New military facilities.
* Improved spacecraft.
* Exploration expeditions.
* Recovery of alien technology.
* Human forces expanding beyond protected territories.
* New outposts and forward operating bases.

The visual atmosphere should gradually feel more adventurous and optimistic.

## 8. GLOBAL COLOUR PALETTE

Establish recognizable visual identities while allowing individual environments to maintain their own colours.

### Humanity

Primary visual direction:

* Warm whites.
* Muted gold.
* Steel blue.
* Military greys.
* Natural earth tones.

Humanity should feel advanced, practical and technologically sophisticated.

### Alien imperial remnants

Primary visual direction:

* Metallic blue.
* Cool violet.
* Distinctive faction-specific accents.
* Unusual energy illumination.
* Species-specific materials and surface treatments.

Do not force every alien faction to use purple. Each species must have a distinctive identity.

### Uncharted environments

Use natural environmental colours:

* Vibrant greens.
* Golden yellows.
* Oceanic blues.
* Earthy browns.
* Distinctive mineral colours.

Avoid excessive saturation. Aim for cinematic colour grading and natural colour relationships.

## 9. WEATHER AND ATMOSPHERIC SYSTEMS

Implement environmental variety through dynamic weather.

Examples:

* Clear sunny skies.
* Cloudy weather.
* Rain.
* Thunderstorms.
* Snowfall.
* Snowstorms.
* Sandstorms.
* Fog in appropriate environments.
* Atmospheric dust.
* Alien weather phenomena.

Weather should affect the lighting, visibility, materials and atmosphere naturally.

Do not use fog or storms as permanent substitutes for environmental detail.

Clear weather should remain common enough that players regularly experience beautiful scenery.

## 10. ENVIRONMENTAL DETAIL AND AAA PRESENTATION

Every environment must feel deliberately constructed rather than procedurally assembled from repetitive objects.

Prioritize:

* Large-scale environmental composition.
* Strong silhouettes.
* Interesting landmarks.
* Realistic terrain transitions.
* Sophisticated architecture.
* Detailed surface materials.
* Natural vegetation distribution.
* Environmental storytelling.
* Carefully designed points of interest.
* Realistic scale relationships.
* High-quality distant scenery.

Buildings should look complete, engineered and intentionally designed.

Avoid structures that look unfinished, randomly assembled or like basic construction blocks.

Procedural generation must preserve artistic quality and composition.

## 11. GAMEPLAY READABILITY

Atmosphere must never interfere with gameplay.

Ensure:

* Enemies remain visually distinguishable from backgrounds.
* Important objects are identifiable.
* Combat areas have appropriate visibility.
* Shadows do not conceal important gameplay information unnecessarily.
* Lighting does not obscure weapon effects.
* Environmental effects do not overwhelm the screen.
* Different alien species are recognizable at a distance.

Maintain a balance between cinematic presentation and competitive shooter readability.

## 12. TECHNICAL IMPLEMENTATION

Translate this art direction into the existing game architecture.

Before implementing:

1. Inspect the current rendering pipeline.
2. Identify existing lighting systems.
3. Inspect materials, shaders and post-processing.
4. Identify existing environment-generation systems.
5. Identify performance bottlenecks.
6. Determine which existing systems should be improved rather than replaced.

Implement the improvements in a modular, scalable manner.

Prioritize:

* Physically based rendering.
* High-quality material response.
* Consistent exposure.
* Realistic indirect illumination.
* Dynamic time-of-day systems where appropriate.
* Scalable shadows.
* Atmospheric rendering.
* High-quality sky and cloud systems.
* Optimized vegetation and environmental assets.
* Consistent colour grading.

Maintain good performance across large environments.

Do not sacrifice gameplay performance for unnecessary visual effects.

## 13. FINAL CREATIVE RULES

These rules take priority throughout the project:

1. The universe must feel alive, not dead.
2. Exploration must feel exciting and rewarding.
3. Beautiful environments must exist alongside dangerous encounters.
4. Darkness should be intentional, not the default.
5. Human civilization must communicate hope and resilience.
6. Every alien species must have a recognizable visual identity.
7. The fractured empire must be reflected in the environment.
8. Planetary environments must have substantial visual diversity.
9. Lighting must be realistic, cinematic and gameplay-friendly.
10. Procedural generation must produce intentionally designed-looking environments, not repetitive generic landscapes.

## FINAL OBJECTIVE

Create a visually spectacular, immersive and cohesive science-fiction universe that players genuinely want to explore.

The game should communicate the feeling of an enormous civilization recovering from an interstellar catastrophe while an entire alien empire collapses into competing factions.

It should feel like an ongoing universe with its own history, politics, civilizations, conflicts and unexplored frontiers.

The intended atmosphere is hopeful military science fiction with breathtaking environments, realistic cinematic lighting, monumental architecture, advanced technology and a sense of endless discovery.

Do not simply make the game darker to make it look more realistic. Make it more detailed, more sophisticated, more atmospheric and more beautiful.

## Notes: how this brief meets the story and the other rules (ours, not the owner's)

**The story** (`docs/story/WORLD.md`, the owner's of 2026-11-14) and this brief tell the same history: a
many-species empire (the Vanthos Empire) takes Sol world by world, the fight reaches the sky over Earth, the emperor
is murdered, the species turn on each other, most of the fleet leaves, what stays fights its neighbours for Sol,
and humanity rebuilds on stolen technology (void crystal) and goes out to take the system back. Kept from the story
in everything this brief asks for: no dates or lengths of time for the war (the brief's four phases are stages, not
dates); nobody on Earth knows why the empire turned (the world SHOWS the fracture, the fleets fighting each other,
without explaining it: the emperor's murder stays the campaign's secret).

What this brief ADDS to the story, now part of the direction:
- Factions that are neutral toward humanity, and some that may become allies (the story had every species wanting
  Sol for itself). None is designed yet; each species is still designed only when the owner asks for its place.
- Places where two species fight each other (example 1 of section 6). The story's rule "a place held by one species
  has only that species in it" still holds for a HELD place (Meridian: only the Sarketh); a contested place, a
  frontier between two species, is another kind of place, not yet in the game. Traces of other factions (another
  species' wrecks, a captured installation under a different insignia) can appear anywhere as storytelling.
- The war visible in the world: the story noted that nothing in the levels shows it yet (the fallen station, the
  wrecks of the fleet). This brief makes that a requirement (sections 6 and 7).

**Earth.** Nexus Prime is the protected human city of section 5 (raised round a grounded Vanthos carrier whose heart
powers it: recovered alien technology in human infrastructure); Meridian is outside the walls. Its plan
(`tools/city/nexus_plan.py`) already has the great wall (about 8.4 x 5.4 km inside it, four gates), a refuge park and
parks in its districts, hangars in the military district and the dockyard; whether they LOOK as section 5 asks has
not been checked against it. "Populated": the standing rule of 2026-11-07 keeps people and vehicles OUT of Nexus
Prime until the owner asks for them, so that stays until the owner says so.

**Meridian today** (`make_operation_map.py`, checked 2026-11-16) is built as "a storm night": a low warm light under
the storm, dense height fog with volumetrics over the whole operation (density 0.026), exposure held down, bloom
1.1, a heavy vignette (0.45), film grain, a chromatic fringe, a cool, desaturated grade, and violet crystal, rim and
beam lights. Against this brief it is the place most out of line: fog and storm as the permanent state, lens effects
pushed, darkness as the default, violet that belongs to no species of the current story.

**The operations' rules** (`docs/operation/ART_DIRECTION.md`, 2026-11-10) ask Meridian for "a dark, mysterious
atmosphere" that never hides the enemies, sunlight between the towers on some streets and alien light on others.
This brief makes darkness intentional and never the default, and the world beautiful even where it is dangerous.
Read together: Meridian's darkness is that operation's intention, and it must still have sun, colour, beauty and
readable fights; the storm round the array's beam is the story's (`docs/story/MERIDIAN_FALL.md`: nothing flies
through it), not a permanent stand-in for detail. Where the two differ and it matters, the owner rules.

**Colour.** Humanity: warm whites, muted gold, steel blue, military greys, earth tones. Each species its own: the
Sarketh's is theirs (pale shells over a dark frame, cyan light, amber crystalline slugs, red cloth with their
emblem, a little gold); violet is reserved for another species (the Void Requiem set's), and the brief says not to
make every faction purple. Open: Meridian's alien growth still glows violet (`M_SOGlowPulse`, the "Choir" spires and
hive growths from the first story), which belongs to no species of the current story.

**Names.** The games this brief names are a quality reference only: no name, term or text from another game goes
into ours (a hard rule).

**Procedural generation.** The game builds its places from plans and scripts (Nexus Prime from
`tools/city/nexus_plan.py` at runtime, Meridian from the Blender scripts); there is no planet or biome generator. The
biome families of section 4 are the direction for places still to come; any generator built for them must produce
places that look designed (section 10, rule 10).

**Section 12** (inspect the rendering pipeline, lighting, materials and post-processing, environment generation and
performance before changing them) has not been done against this brief yet.
